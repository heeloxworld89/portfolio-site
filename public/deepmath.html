The takeaway

Small repairs keep training stable. Only a jump can revive a dead layer.

We prove both, which makes self-repair a switched system: bounded flicks between isolated jumps.

Three numbers

  • 10.00%without repair, after two conv layers are zeroed: exactly chance, 99 epochs, 3/3 seeds.
  • 39 493the training step where the first reset fires, identical in every seed.
  • 80.3%with self-repair (85% before the lesion).

Two results

Theorem 1

Bounded repair is safe (practical ISS)

Near a minimum θ∗\theta^*θ∗, with LLL-smooth, α\alphaα-strongly convex loss, step η≤1/L\eta \le 1/Lη≤1/L, repairs ∥ut∥≤εmax⁡\lVert u_t \rVert \le \varepsilon_{\max}∥ut​∥≤εmax​, and local gradients (weight β\betaβ) bounded by MMM:

lim sup⁡t→∞ ∥θt−θ∗∥≤γ=2βMα+εmax⁡η α\limsup_{t \to \infty}\, \lVert \theta_t - \theta^* \rVert \le \gamma = \frac{2\beta M}{\alpha} + \frac{\varepsilon_{\max}}{\eta\,\alpha}t→∞limsup​∥θt​−θ∗∥≤γ=α2βM​+ηαεmax​​

Repairs push training around, but never further than γ\gammaγ from a good solution.

Theorem 2

Bounded repair cannot revive (absorbing dead set)

In a Conv–BN–ReLU layer (BatchNorm in train mode), a channel with zero filter and BN shift βc≤0\beta_c \le 0βc​≤0 stays dead under AdamW and under any repair with ∥R(W)−W∥≤ρ ∥W∥\lVert R(W) - W \rVert \le \rho\, \lVert W \rVert∥R(W)−W∥≤ρ∥W∥.

Gradient descent and small fixes are both stuck, exactly. Only a jump leaves.

The takeaway

Small repairs keep training stable. Only a jump can revive a dead layer.

We prove both, which makes self-repair a switched system: bounded flicks between isolated jumps.

Abstract

Methods that repair a network while it trains, such as Continual Backprop, ReDo, Shrink & Perturb and plasticity injection, apply updates that are not the gradient of any loss, so the usual convergence proofs do not cover them. We model each repair as an input to the training dynamics and ask for input-to-state stability (ISS). Proposition 1 shows that near a strongly convex minimum, bounded repairs keep training within a radius γ = 2βM/α + ε̄/(ηα) of the minimum, where β ≤ 0.3 weights each layer's local loss, M bounds that loss's gradient, and ε̄ is the asymptotic repair size. Theorem 2 gives the matching limit: in a Conv–BN–ReLU layer, a channel with a zero filter and non-positive BatchNorm shift is invariant under AdamW and under every per-channel-bounded repair, so only a reset can revive it. Self-repairing training is therefore a switched system: bounded corrections between isolated jumps. We test both predictions in a 383-run evaluation. After a shock, the largest correction per epoch falls by 93% (5.07 → 0.38); a CIFAR-10 CNN with two zeroed conv layers stays at exactly 10.00% under standard AdamW and recovers to 80.3% with self-repair; on ResNet-18 the skip path makes repair unnecessary.

Keywords: input-to-state stability · switched systems · loss of plasticity · self-repairing training · BatchNorm

Open problem

How often may a network jump?

Proposition 1 holds between jumps; Theorem 2 says jumps are unavoidable. Find a dwell time τD\tau_DτD​ such that jumps at least τD\tau_DτD​ apart keep training stable (cf. Hespanha & Morse, 1999).

Also open: strong convexity cannot hold exactly for BatchNorm layers (scale invariance); Cui et al. (2025) give ISS under a generalised PL condition, but whether BN networks satisfy it is open.

If you have ideas, I'd love to hear them.

Questions I'd expect at the poster

Isn't Theorem 2 just the dead-ReLU problem?

The mechanism is simple: a zero filter gives a constant pre-activation, train-mode BatchNorm maps it to βc ≤ 0, and ReLU blocks every gradient. What the theorem adds is the exact conditions: fresh optimiser state, and invariance under every per-channel-bounded repair, not only gradient steps. That makes it checkable, which is what ormas-monitor certifies, and it is the lower bound that makes self-repair a switched system.

You zeroed the layers yourself. Does this happen in real models?

The lesion is a controlled test, so exact zeros are by construction. Scanning 22 released torchvision ImageNet models, I find channels decayed to about zero (filter or BatchNorm scale below 10−5 of the layer median, shift ≤ 0) in 19 of the 21 that use BatchNorm → ReLU. They are not exactly zero, so the theorem does not certify them. On 64 real images (CIFAR-10 test images upscaled to 224 px) they output exactly 0 in ResNet-18, VGG-19-BN and MnasNet, and removing them from VGG-19-BN cuts compute by 18.6% with identical top-1 answers. Shrinking BatchNorm scales is also a known pruning technique (network slimming); the point is that one command finds them. Full results

With no repair, γ is still 2βM/α. Why doesn't training converge to θ*?

Because training also optimises each layer's local loss with weight β ≤ 0.3, which pulls against the global loss. That offset is why this is practical ISS; it becomes strict ISS only if β(t) → 0. (β here is the local-loss weight, not the BatchNorm shift βc in Theorem 2.)

Why model a repair as an input rather than as part of the optimiser?

Because it is not a gradient step. A repair fires when a diagnostic crosses a threshold, depends on the current state and is discontinuous. Writing training as θt+1 = θt − η∇L(θt) + ut lets input-to-state stability bound the effect of ut without requiring it to descend any objective.

How does this relate to Cui, Jiang, Sontag & Braatz (2025)?

They prove that perturbed gradient descent is small-disturbance ISS under a generalised Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition. Modelling repair as the input lets bounds of that kind apply to self-repair. What this work adds is the repair-specific side: the diagnostic-triggered input, the measurable condition the bound needs (repairs must shrink), and Theorem 2, which shows that bounded repair alone cannot leave a dead set.

Strong convexity cannot hold with BatchNorm. Doesn't that break Proposition 1?

It makes Proposition 1 a local, idealised statement, and the poster says so: BatchNorm's scale invariance rules out exact strong convexity. The natural replacement is a PL-type condition, under which Cui et al. (2025) already have ISS for gradient descent; whether BN networks satisfy it is the open step. Theorem 2 does not use convexity at all.

Why exactly 10.00%, with zero variance across seeds?

Once the layers are zeroed, the network's output no longer depends on the input, so it predicts the same class for every image. The CIFAR-10 test set is balanced, 1,000 images per class, so accuracy is exactly 10.00%. Theorem 2 says no gradient step or bounded repair can change that, and none did in 99 epochs across 3 seeds.

Does Theorem 2 depend on the optimiser state?

Yes. Leftover momentum could move a zero filter, so the theorem is stated for AdamW with fresh state for the channel. Every gradient into the channel is then exactly zero, and with decoupled weight decay the BatchNorm shift evolves as βc(t) = (1 − ηλ)t βc(0), which stays non-positive; the run logs match this to the bit. The ormas-monitor certificate checks these conditions before it certifies a channel as dead.

Why doesn't ResNet-18 benefit from repair?

The skip path routes around the same lesion, so the network barely notices it: 92.3% without repair against 91.6% with (3-seed means). Repair matters when there is no redundancy to fall back on.

Is the bound informative in practice?

Only if ε̄ is small, which is what makes it a prediction rather than a formality: a self-repairing network should need progressively smaller repairs. It does: after a shock the largest correction per epoch falls from 5.07 to 0.38 (Figure 2).

Reproduce it

Theorem 2 is implemented as a check you can run on your own PyTorch model: ormas-monitor watches training, certifies dead channels when the theorem's conditions hold, and can optionally repair them.

# the lesion experiment, small scale
pip install "ormas-monitor[demo]"
ormas-monitor demo

The demo trains a CIFAR-10 CNN, zeroes two of its three conv layers and clears the optimiser state, then trains on twice from that same state: once only watching, once repairing. It downloads CIFAR-10 (about 170 MB) once.
